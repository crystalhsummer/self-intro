By session fifteen, without notes:

Mandarin, first block

Fifteen sessions. Everything on this page is something you will use in a room, not something you will be tested on.

How this works

Four things worth knowing before session one, so the design makes sense as you go.

  • The target is ILR 1, narrowTwelve months of daily practice gets you to introductions, courtesies, and following the shape of a conversation in your own field. Not negotiating. Anyone promising more is selling.
  • Ten minutes daily beats ninety weeklyYou already know this from the keyboard. Same reason, same mechanism.
  • You will not write charactersRecognition only, about sixty simplified and fifteen traditional. Handwriting has the lowest return of anything available to you, so we are skipping it on purpose.
  • Colour is tone, all the way downCharacter, pinyin, and the English underneath it all carry the same colour. Red level, amber rising, green low, blue falling, grey neutral. Follow one colour across the three lines and you can see which English word belongs to which Chinese one.

Daily warm-up

Five minutes, every day, indefinitely. Say it out loud. Silent reading does nothing here.

The four tones

mā
LevelSustained, no movement. A held note.
má
RisingPlay an ascending fifth, then sing it as one glide rather than two notes. That glide is the tone.
mǎ
LowStay low and flat. The textbook dip is rare in real speech. Learn the flat version first.
mà
FallingSharp fall from high, short. Staccato.

Tone pairs

Speech is made of pairs, not isolated syllables. This is the drill that actually moves you.

Drill

English appears, you say it in Mandarin out loud, then reveal and play it back. Wrong is fine. Silent is not.

Press start.

Audio uses your device's built-in Mandarin voice. If nothing plays, your phone or computer has no Chinese voice installed. On iOS add one under Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, Voices. On Android, under Settings, Text-to-speech.

What you can say

Three lines per sentence: characters, sound, meaning. The pinyin stays on the page permanently, because the point is to get the sentence out of your mouth, not to decode it. The English sits in Chinese word order, so the gaps and inversions you see are the grammar.

Take-home practice

Ten minutes a day, six days a week. Every week ends with one thing you bring to the session and say out loud. That is the only homework there is.
